# Extension activities: Markov chains in continuous time

The M/M/1 queue that has been studied in my example report is the simplest model of a queue in queing theory.  
There are a vast number of models that can be studied using the simple principles that have been introduced here.
For example:

* You can consider an M/M/1 queue with finite capacity.
* You can consider an M/D/1 queue where the service time for each customer is fixed.
* You can consider an M/G/1 queue where the service time for each customer is not simply an exponential random variable.
* You could consider an G/G/1 queue where the arrival times between customers and the service times are not simply exponential random variables.
* You could consider an M/M/n (or M/D/n, M/G/n or G/G/n) queue which has multiple servers dealing with customers.
* You could have multiple types of customer coming into the queue some who take $1/\mu_1$~s to get served and others who take $1/mu_2$~s to get served.

The modelling of the M/M/1 queue that I have introduced here serves as an introduction to a broader framework called event-based modelling.  
This framework, which uses random variables to generate the times between key events, is one that can be powerfully applied to many different
categories of problem. There is thus lots of scope for creativity and for perhaps even doing something useful for a stakeholder using these
ideas.
