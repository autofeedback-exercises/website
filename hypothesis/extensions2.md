# Suggested extensions: Understanding test stastics

In the example report that accompanied the exercises on this topic I investigated the statistical distribution that is 
sampled by the test statistic that is used when you perform one-sided and two-sided statistical tests to determine if the 
population mean from a distribution is greater than $\mu$ or not equal to $\mu$ resptively.

In my example report, my samples from of the test statstic were samples from a probability distribution where the expectation
was equal to $\mu$. The simplest thing you could do to extend this work would be to sample from a distribution that has an expectation
that is not equal to $\mu$. Alternatively, your samples of the statistics could be generated using a random variable that is different
from the normal random variables that were used in my example report.  However, notice that in SOR1020 you also learned about the 
following statistical tests:

* Comparison of means
* Tests of proportions
* Comparison of two proportions
* Tests of correlation coefficients
* Paired comparison tests (you learned multiple methods for doing this)
* Tests of slope and intercept in linear regression models
* Comparison of two variances
* Comparison of $n>2$ means
* Comparison of unkown variance with known variance
* Goodness-of-fit Test for Classified Data
* Sign test - single median
* Wilcoxon signed rank test - single median or mean
* Mann-Whitney Test
* Run Test for Randomness

Looking through your SOR1020 notes will allow you to understand the theory that underpins each of these tests. Understanding that theory 
will give you ideas for generating model data that you can use to investigate the distribution that is sampled by test statistic that is 
used in each one of these statistical tests. In other words, you can something similar to what I did in the example report for 
Student's t-distribution for some other distribution.
